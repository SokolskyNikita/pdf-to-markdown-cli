## THE PRONOUN.

### § 32. *The Personal Pronoun. The Separate Pronoun.*

1. The personal pronoun (as well as the pronoun generally) *a* belongs to the oldest and simplest elements of the language (§ 30. 5). It should be considered *before* the verb, since it plays an important part in verbal inflexion (§§ 44, 47).

### II. Second Person.

*f* 4. The forms of the 2nd person אַתָּה, אַתָּ, אַתֶּם, אַתֶּנָּה, &c., are contracted from *anta*, &c. The kindred languages have retained the *n* before the א, e.g. Arab. 'anti, fem. 'anti, thou; pl. 'antum, fem. 'antunna, ye. In Syriac אַתָּה, fem. אַתָּנָה are written, but both are pronounced 'at. In Western Aramaic אַתָּנָה is usual for both genders.

*g* אַתָּה (without ה) occurs five times, e.g. Ps. 6, 4, always as אַתָּה תִּחְיֶה, with אַתָּה as אַתָּה. In three places אַתָּה appears as a masculine, Num. 11, 15. Deut. 5, 24. Ezek. 28, 14.

*h* The feminine form was originally אַתָּנָה as in Syriac, Arabic and Ethiopic. This form is found seven times as אַתָּנָה תִּחְיֶה (Jud. 17, 2. 1 Ki. 14, 2. 2 Ki. 4, 16. 23, 8. 1. Jer. 4, 30. Ezek. 36, 13) and appears also in the corresponding personal ending of verbs (see § 44. *f*) especially, and necessarily, before suffixes, as אַתָּנָה תִּחְיֶה, § 59. 1, *c*; comp. also *f* as the ending of the 2nd fem. sing. of the Imperative and Imperfect.
